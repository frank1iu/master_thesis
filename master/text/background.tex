\section{Baseline Round-to-Nearest Quantization}

The baseline for post-training quantization is symmetric uniform quantization using round-to-nearest (RTN) rounding. \cite{nagelWhitePaperNeural2021b}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Baseline RTN Quantization}\label{alg:rtn_quant}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $W$ (unquantized weights), $b$ (target bit width)
\Ensure Quantized model $W_q$
\State $q_{\text{min}} \gets -2^{b - 1}$
\State $q_{\text{max}} \gets 2^{b - 1} - 1$
\State $\text{scale} \gets \frac{\max(W)}{q_{\text{max}}}$
\State $W_q \gets \text{clamp}\left(\lceil \frac{W}{\text{scale}} \rfloor, q_\text{min}, q_\text{max}\right)$
\State \Return $W_q$, scale
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Baseline RTN Dequantization}\label{alg:rtn_dequant}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $W_q$ (quantized weights), scale
\Ensure Dequantized weights $\hat{W}$
\State $\hat{W} \gets W_q \cdot \text{scale}$
\State \Return $\hat{W}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Small Language Models}

In this section, we will examine TinyStories~\cite{eldanTinyStoriesHowSmall2023d}, a work that pushes the boundaries of small language models, and provide a few custom language models for further experimentation. By using a low-variance synthetic dataset (children's stories) generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 containing only words a typical 3-4 year old would understand, they were able to generate coherent output using models with as few as 1 million parameters.

One limitation we observed in this work is their usage of the GPT-Neo~\cite{blackGPTNeoLargeScale2021} tokenizer, restricted to the top 10,000 tokens. Using a generic tokenizer for a low-vocabulary dataset like TinyStories might result in wasted weights (tokens that never appear in TinyStories), and might not provide the optimal tokenzation for our specialized dataset. 

As a preliminary experiment, we train two small language models on TinyStories v1 and TinyStories v2, respectively. The original paper only provided models trained on TinyStories v1. We then evaluate their accuracy using perplexity on the validation datasets as a metric.
