\chapter{AdaRound Inversion Algorithm} \label{chap:adaround_inversion}
In this chapter, we propose and evaluate a novel algorithm to reconstruct representations of training data in AdaRound.

\section{Formulation of Leakage Channel}
AdaRound's objective function is expected to approach zero after training. Let us define the weight perturbation matrix as $\Delta W = W - \hat{W}$. The objective function can be rewritten as follows.
\begin{align}
\|Wx - \hat{W}x\|_F^2 = \|\Delta W x\|_F^2
\end{align}
After optimization, we expect the following.
\begin{align}
\|\Delta W x\|_F^2 \approx 0 \iff \Delta W x \approx 0
\end{align}

We notice that $\Delta W x = 0 \implies x \in \mathcal{N}(\Delta W)$. Therefore, the training data $x$ used to calibrate the AdaRound process approximately lies within the null space of the weight perturbation matrix $\Delta W$. 

\section{Method}
An attacker with access to the original weights $W$ and the quantized weights $\hat{W}$ can generate a sample $\hat{x}$ that lies within this null space. We can initialize a sample $\hat{x}$ from random noise and apply Stochastic Gradient Descent with the objective $\arg\min_{\hat{x}} \|\Delta W \hat{x}\|_2^2$, as described in Algorithm \ref{alg:inversion}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Proposed Inversion Method}\label{alg:inversion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $W$, $\hat{W}$, $\hat{x}_{\text{init}}$, Number of Iterations $K$, Learning Rate $\eta$
\State $\Delta W \gets W - \hat{W}$
\State $\hat{x} \gets \hat{x}_\text{init}$
\For{$k = 1$ \textbf{to} $K$}
    \State $\mathcal{L}  \gets \sum \left( \Delta W  \tanh(\hat{x}) \right)^2$ (Forward pass)
    \State $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \hat{x}} \gets \text{Autograd}(\mathcal{L}, \hat{x})$ (Backward pass via autograd~\cite{paszkePyTorchImperativeStyle2019})
    \State $\hat{x} \gets \hat{x} - \eta \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\hat{x}}}$ (Update $\hat{x}$)
\EndFor
\State \Return $\tanh(\hat{x})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Note that the objective can be rewritten as follows.
\begin{align}
\|\Delta W \hat{x}\|_2^2 = (\Delta W \hat{x})^T(\Delta W \hat{x}) = \hat{x}^T (\Delta W^T \Delta W) \hat{x}
\end{align}
A simple optimization can therefore be made by computing the Gram matrix $G = \Delta W^T \Delta W$ once, then using it to calculate the objective as $\hat{x}^TG\hat{x}$ in each iteration. (See Algorithm \ref{alg:inversion_optimized})

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Proposed Inversion with Optimization}\label{alg:inversion_optimized}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $W$, $\hat{W}$, $\hat{x}_{\text{init}}$, Number of Iterations $K$, Learning Rate $\eta$
\State $\Delta W \gets W - \hat{W}$
\State $\hat{x} \gets \hat{x}_\text{init}$
\State $G \gets \Delta W^T \Delta W$
\For{$k = 1$ \textbf{to} $K$}
    \State $\mathcal{L}  \gets \sum \left( \tanh(\hat{x})^TG  \tanh(\hat{x}) \right)$ (Forward pass)
    \State $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \hat{x}} \gets \text{Autograd}(\mathcal{L}, \hat{x})$ (Backward pass via autograd~\cite{paszkePyTorchImperativeStyle2019})
    \State $\hat{x} \gets \hat{x} - \eta \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\hat{x}}}$ (Update $\hat{x}$)
\EndFor
\State \Return $\tanh(\hat{x})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Evaluation} \label{sec:adaround_inversion_experiment_setup}
\subsection{Experiment Setup}
We trained MLPs on these datasets: Olivetti Faces~\cite{JMLR:v12:pedregosa11a}, Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia)~\cite{kermanyIdentifyingMedicalDiagnoses2018}, and the Weizmann Horse Database~\cite{weizmannHorseDB}, on a single computer (CPU: Intel Xeon w9-3495X, GPU: 1x NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090). We perform 500 iterations of AdaRound quantization using the Adam optimizer, then obtain $\Delta W$, the difference between the quantized and the original model. For each model, we generate 10 random samples from a Gaussian distribution, then train for 3000 iterations using the Adam optimizer. We report the SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure), PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), and Normalized RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) between the mean of the calibration data and the mean of the 10 generated samples before and after our inversion method in Table \ref{tab:adaround_inversion_metrics}. We also report a visualization of the mean calibration sample vs. the mean generated sample in Figures \ref{fig:adaround_inversion_olivetti}, \ref{fig:adaround_inversion_xray}, and \ref{fig:adaround_inversion_horse}.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Main Experiment}
From the metrics, we observe an increase in SSIM and PSNR, and a decrease in Normalized RMSE after training. In the images, we can see the silhouettes and outlines of the calibration set, although it is quite difficult to make out. From the results, we can conclude that the $\Delta W$ of a model quantized using AdaRound does contain information about the calibration data. 

\subsection{Ablation Study}
Interestingly, we see a slight improvement in PSNR and NRMSE in the ablation study. However, crucially the SSIM shows that round-to-nearest quantization is not vulnerable to our method. In other words, our method indeed extracts information about the calibration data in data-dependent quantization methods.

\subsection{Overall}
We demonstrate that AdaRound encodes information from the calibration data into the weight perturbation matrix. By targeting the null space of this matrix, our proposed method generates samples that resemble outlines of the calibration data. While the reconstructed data only represents the mean of the calibration data rather than exact instances, the leakage is significant enough to warrant reconsideration of the security of data-dependent PTQ methods. 

\subsection{Limitations}
The most important limitation is the fact that this method only reconstructs representations of the mean of the calibration data, but not individual samples. The low SSIM ($\sim0.14$) is another limitation: this method only shows evidence for the leakage channel but the quality may be improved. For future work, it is possible to consider adding Total Variation (TV) loss~\cite{rudinNonlinearTotalVariation1992}, or using a prior such as a deep image prior~\cite{ulyanovDeepImagePrior2020}, to improve the quality of the reconstructed samples.
